The Will

I asked it to write my will.
It divided everything fairly:
the house, the savings, the car,
each share weighed to the gram.

It even found the watch I'd lost,
in a photograph from 1998,
and gave it to the son
who'd asked for it at twelve.

One thing it could not price:
the bicycle with the bent wheel,
leaning in the shed since the summer
I taught my daughter to ride.

So I wrote that line myself,
in the hand she'll know as mine:
to my daughter, the bicycle.
Not the house. Not the savings.
The afternoon she learned to fall
and get up, laughing, and ride.
